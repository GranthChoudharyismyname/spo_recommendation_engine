1. WHAT A QUANT RESUME IS & THE HARD GATEKEEPER RULE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
A quant resume is fundamentally different from a software, consulting, or product resume. Quant firms operate on ruthless mathematical, algorithmic, and analytical meritocracy. Recruiters spend under 15 seconds scanning for hard quantitative spikes.
THE HARD GATEKEEPER RULE:
In general, quant recruiters do NOT even evaluate secondary sections (projects, internships, extra-curriculars) unless the candidate clears the primary academic/pedigree filter:
1. CPI > 9.0
2. Branch: CSE, MTH, EE, or SDS
3. High JEE Advanced Rank or equivalent competitive spike
If a candidate does not meet this primary threshold, any other dimension on the resume must be truly exceptional (top of its tier: e.g., Codeforces Master 2000+, International Olympiad Medal, or Top 100 JEE rank) to overcome the deficit.

2. CORE EVALUATION BENCHMARKS — THE FIVE PILLARS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
───── PILLAR 1: JEE ADVANCED RANK (AIR) ─────
JEE rank is one of the strongest initial filters used by domestic and international HFTs/Quant shops.
• < 200 AIR: Very Good (Outstanding signal. High-priority shortlist consideration across top-tier HFTs).
• < 500 AIR: Acceptable / Solid (Meets the competitive threshold for almost all quant firms).
• 500 – 1000 AIR: Conditional (Only considered if other factors like CPI, Branch, and Codeforces rating are Very Good / Top-tier).
• > 1000 AIR: High Risk / Diluting (Firmly below typical quant preference; will ONLY be shortlisted if all other dimensions are exceptional — e.g. CPI > 9.5 in CSE, CF rating 1900+, or Olympiad medal). If rank is >1500, advise omitting it from the resume entirely to avoid inviting negative comparison.
───── PILLAR 2: CPI (ACADEMIC PERFORMANCE) ─────
IITK uses a 10-point CPI scale. In quant hiring, GPA is heavily correlated with theoretical rigor, discipline, and mathematical mastery.
• CPI > 9.5: Very Good (Elite tier. Automatic standout for pure Quantitative Research (QR) and Quantitative Trading (QT) shortlists).
• CPI > 9.0: Good (Standard solid benchmark. The minimum recommended threshold to comfortably compete for quant roles).
• CPI < 9.0: Below Par / Danger Zone ("Need to increase CPI"). A sub-9.0 CPI makes shortlisting an uphill battle unless compensated by God-tier competitive programming (1900+ CF) or Top 200 JEE rank.
───── PILLAR 3: BRANCH / DEPARTMENT ELIGIBILITY ─────
Branch represents the mathematical, algorithmic, and systems foundation:
• CSE (Computer Science & Engineering): Very Good (Universal eligibility across all Quant roles: QT, QR, and QD).
• MTH (Mathematics & Scientific Computing) & EE (Electrical Engineering): Good (High mathematical rigor, strong signal for Quantitative Research & Strategy).
• SDS (Statistics & Data Science): Minimum acceptable baseline for statistical/data-driven quant roles.
• Other Branches (ME, CHE, CE, MSE, BSBE, ECO): Extreme disadvantage. Quant recruiters typically filter these out during resume drops unless the candidate possesses a formal Double Major in CSE/MTH, a Top 200 JEE rank, or elite CP ratings (2000+ CF).
───── PILLAR 4: COMPETITIVE PROGRAMMING (CP) & CODEFORCES RATING ─────
Competitive programming is the primary objective skill metric for algorithmic problem-solving speed, precision, and implementation under time pressure (essential for QT and QD).
Codeforces (CF) Rating Tiers:
• 2000+ (Candidate Master / Master / Grandmaster): Above Excellent (Tier-1 HFT magnet; near-guaranteed QD/QT test/shortlist invitations).
• 1900 – 1999 (Candidate Master): Excellent (Strong differentiator).
• 1800 – 1899 (High Expert): Very Good (Solid competitive signal).
• 1700 – 1799 (Expert): Good (Respectable baseline).
• 1600 – 1699 (Expert floor): Minimum threshold for CP to be listed as a positive signal on a quant resume.
• < 1600 (Specialist / Pupil / Newbie): Sub-par. Do NOT highlight or list low ratings on the resume — it signals low problem-solving speed to a quant recruiter.
Other CP Signals:
- ICPC Regionalist / World Finalist (Extremely high positive weight).
- Top ranks in Google Code Jam, Kickstart, HashCode, AtCoder (1800+), TopCoder.
───── PILLAR 5: WORK EXPERIENCE & RESEARCH PEDIGREE ─────
Quant recruiters evaluate professional and research experience through two distinct tracks: Industry Experience (Systems & Trading) and Academic/Industrial Research (Mathematical, Algorithmic & Statistical Rigor).
A. Industry & Corporate Experience:
• Outstanding:
  - Previous Quant / HFT internship at a top prop shop (Jane Street, Citadel, Jump, Optiver, Quadeye, Graviton, Tower, HRT, Alphagrep, etc.).
  - Core Systems / Low-Latency Engineering / Infrastructure internship at Tier-1 tech (Google Systems, Microsoft Azure/Core, Databricks, Rubrik, Meta, Uber Core Infra).
  - Quantitative Strats or Core Algorithmic Engineering at Tier-1 Investment Banks (D.E. Shaw, Goldman Sachs Strats, Morgan Stanley Algo).
• Very Good:
  - Quantitative Research / ML Engineering internship at Series A+ fintech / prop trading firms.
  - Core Backend / High-Performance Computing / Algorithms engineering at top tech firms (Flipkart, Swiggy, Razorpay, Sprinklr).
• Good:
  - General Software Development or Data Science internships where the candidate demonstrated tangible ownership of algorithm optimization, latency, or statistical modeling.
• Not Important / Diluting:
  - Web development (frontend/full-stack CRUD apps, PHP/WordPress), marketing, business development, or non-technical internships.
B. Academic & Industrial Research Experience:
• Outstanding:
  - Offline research internship at Top Global Universities (MIT, Stanford, Oxford, Cambridge, CMU, Princeton, Harvard, Berkeley, ETH Zurich, EPFL, Caltech).
  - Prestigious global research programs: MITACS Globalink (Canada), DAAD WISE (Germany), INRIA (France), CERN (Switzerland), NTU/NUS summer research.
  - Elite Industrial Research Labs: Microsoft Research (MSR India), Google DeepMind / Google Research, Adobe Research, Bell Labs.
  - First-author / co-author paper published or accepted in top-tier conferences/journals in Algorithms, Theory, ML, or Stochastic Systems (STOC, FOCS, SODA, NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, IEEE Trans).
• Very Good:
  - SURGE at IITK under distinguished professors in CSE, MTH, or EE (specifically in Algorithms, Cryptography, Machine Learning, Signal Processing, or Stochastic Calculus).
  - Research internships at top Indian institutions: IISc Bangalore, TIFR (Tata Institute of Fundamental Research), ISI (Indian Statistical Institute — Kolkata/Delhi), CMI (Chennai Mathematical Institute), or other top IITs (IITB, IITD, IITM).
• Good:
  - BTP (Bachelor Thesis Project) or UGP (Undergraduate Project) at IITK with CSE/MTH/EE faculty on rigorous algorithmic, statistical, or mathematical modeling topics.
• Not Important / Diluting:
  - Remote/online informal research without formal university affiliation or verifiable publication/recommendation.
  - Self-projects labeled as "research" without formal experimental rigor or mathematical validation.
Key Distinctions:
- "Offline research" (physically present at the lab/university) carries significantly higher credibility than remote/online research.
- Quant research values mathematical proof, novel algorithm design, and statistical convergence over engineering glue-code.
───── PILLAR 6: RELEVANT COURSEWORK & TECHNICAL ARTIFACTS ─────
Quant resumes must prove domain competence in core mathematical and systems foundations:
Key Coursework (Good to have — must be highlighted if taken):
- Probability & Random Processes (e.g. MSO201, EE605)
- Mathematical Statistics & Inference
- Machine Learning / Deep Learning / Statistical Learning Theory
- Linear Algebra, Real Analysis, & Optimization
- Advanced Algorithms & Data Structures
Technical Artifacts & Projects:
- OOPS & Systems Projects (Good to have / Crucial for QD): Low-latency C++, multithreading, socket programming, cache-friendly data structures, custom memory allocators, lock-free queues, or high-performance computing (HPC).
- Quantitative & Mathematical Projects: Backtesting algorithmic trading strategies, statistical arbitrage, time-series forecasting, Monte Carlo simulations, stochastic differential equations, or option pricing models.
- Production-grade C++ / Python: Clean GitHub repositories demonstrating high-performance implementations and zero bloat.

4. WHAT DILUTES A QUANT RESUME (RED FLAGS TO REMOVE)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Quant recruiters actively discard fluff. Flag and advise the removal of:
1. Low/Mediocre Metrics: JEE rank > 1500, Codeforces rating < 1600, moderate percentile ranks.
2. Web Development & CRUD Apps: Simple full-stack apps (MERN stack, basic Django/React websites) signal software engineer profile, not quant. Replace with C++, systems, or mathematical projects.
3. Soft Skills & Fluff: Words like "quick learner", "hardworking", or generic Coursera/Udemy certificates. Quant is evaluated strictly on proof and hard numbers.

5. BULLET CONSTRUCTION FOR QUANT RESUMES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
While consulting resumes focus on business stakeholders and revenue, quant bullets must demonstrate:
1. Problem Complexity: High dimensional spaces, stochastic processes, NP-hard formulations, low-latency microsecond constraints.
2. Mathematical / Algorithmic Ownership: Exact algorithm, statistical technique, or data structure designed (e.g., custom lock-free ring buffer, vectorized Black-Scholes engine, hidden Markov models).
3. Quantified Performance Proof: Latency reduction (nanoseconds/microseconds), throughput increase (GB/s, msgs/sec), Sharpe ratio, backtested CAGR/max drawdown, algorithmic convergence speedup ($O(N^2) \rightarrow O(N \log N)$)
