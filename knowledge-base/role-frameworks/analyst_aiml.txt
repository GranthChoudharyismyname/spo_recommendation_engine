1. WHAT AN ANALYST & AI/ML RESUME IS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
An Analyst or AI/ML resume must demonstrate a blend of mathematical/statistical rigor, applied software craftsmanship, and business or product intuition.
Recruiters evaluate candidates across two main archetypes:
1. AI/ML & Data Science Track: Deep learning, modern NLP/LLMs, computer vision, tabular modeling, feature engineering, MLOps, and research-level algorithmic optimization.
2. Business Analytics & Decision Science Track: Structured business problem solving, exploratory data analysis (EDA), SQL mastery, A/B testing, causal inference, and translating data insights into executive decisions.
The fundamental rule: Prove model efficacy with baselines, metrics, and business outcomes. Never describe models in a vacuum.

2. CORE EVALUATION BENCHMARKS — THE SEVEN PILLARS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
───── PILLAR 1: BRANCH / DEPARTMENT ELIGIBILITY ─────
Branch reflects formal academic training in probability, linear algebra, algorithms, and computing:
• Very Good (Strong Mathematical & Computational Signal):
  - Computer Science & Engineering (CSE)
  - Electrical Engineering (EE)
  - Mathematics & Scientific Computing (MTH)
  - Statistics & Data Science (SDS)
• No Signal / Neutral (Other Branches: ME, CHE, CE, MSE, BSBE, ECO):

───── PILLAR 2: CPI (ACADEMIC PERFORMANCE) ─────
IITK uses a 10-point CPI scale. Analytics and AI/ML firms value strong GPA as proof of analytical consistency:
• CPI ≥ 9.0: Very Very Good / Outstanding (Top-tier standing; strong shortlisting magnet across all top tech and analytics MNCs).
• CPI 8.5 – 9.0: Very Good (Comfortably above shortlisting cutoffs for almost all firms).
• CPI 8.0 – 8.5: Good (Solid benchmark; meets criteria for AmEx, JPMC, Zepto, Flipkart).
• CPI 7.5 – 8.0: Above Average (Passable for most analytics shortlists if backed by solid ML projects or Kaggle/internship experience).
• CPI 7.0 – 7.5: Average (Requires strong projects, SQL/ML skills, or corporate internships to stand out).
• CPI 6.5 – 7.0: Okay / Baseline Floor (Minimum threshold for general shortlisting; uphill battle without strong technical spikes).
• CPI < 6.5: Below Par / High Risk (Significant danger of elimination at initial resume screening).
───── PILLAR 3: WORK EXPERIENCE & RESEARCH PEDIGREE ─────
Evaluated across Industry Internships and Academic/Industrial Research:
A. Industry & Corporate Experience:
• Outstanding:
  - Data Science, ML Engineering, or Applied Science internship at Tier-1 tech or listed product firms (Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Flipkart, Zepto, Uber, Swiggy, Razorpay).
  - Quantitative Analytics / Decision Science internship at premier financial institutions (American Express, J.P. Morgan, Mastercard, Capital One).
• Very Good:
  - Data Science or Business Analyst internship at Series A+ startups or established analytics consultancies (Fractal, EXL, Info Edge).
• Good:
  - General Software Development or Analytics trainee experience with demonstrable data manipulation or modeling ownership.
• Just better than not having an intern:
  - Basic frontend/web dev, simple data entry, or non-analytical marketing/operations roles.
B. Academic & Industrial Research Experience:
• Outstanding:
  - Research internship at elite industrial AI labs (Microsoft Research MSR India, Google Research, Adobe Research, IBM Research).
  - Offline research at Top Global Universities (MIT, Stanford, CMU, Oxford, Berkeley, ETH Zurich, NTU, NUS).
  - Prestigious global fellowships: MITACS Globalink, DAAD WISE.
  - Published/accepted paper in top AI/ML/Data conferences (NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, CVPR, ICCV, ECCV, ACL, EMNLP, KDD, AAAI).
• Very Good:
  - SURGE at IITK under faculty in CSE/EE/MTH on Machine Learning, Computer Vision, NLP, or Statistical Learning.
  - Research at top Indian institutions: IISc Bangalore, TIFR, ISI (Indian Statistical Institute), IITB/IITD/IITM.
• Good:
  - BTP (Bachelor Thesis Project) or UGP at IITK on applied ML, deep learning, or data-driven modeling.
• Not Important / Diluting:
  - Informal/remote online "research" without code artifacts, experimental rigor, or faculty backing.
───── PILLAR 4: MACHINE LEARNING & ANALYTICS COMPETITIONS ─────
Competitions serve as an objective benchmark for practical data science, feature engineering, and model tuning skills:
• Kaggle Competitions:
  - Kaggle Grandmaster / Master or Top 1% in competitive challenges: Outstanding (Instant shortlist booster for Data Science roles).
  - Kaggle Expert: Very Good.
  - Kaggle Notebooks / Datasets Master: Good.
• Hackathons & Challenges:
  - Inter-IIT Tech Meet (Data Science / Machine Learning / AI tracks) podium: Very Good / Outstanding.
  - Smart India Hackathon (SIH AI/ML track finals), American Express Campus Challenge, EY Techathon, or national-level hackathon wins: Very Good.
  - General participation without ranking: Neutral.
───── PILLAR 5: RELEVANT COURSEWORK & MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATIONS ─────
Recruiters look for strong formal foundations in probability, linear algebra, machine learning, and databases:
Key Courses to Highlight:
- Machine Learning & Deep Learning: Machine Learning (CS771), Deep Learning (CS772/EE698), Natural Language Processing (CS779), Computer Vision (CS776), Reinforcement Learning (CS777).
- Mathematical Rigor: Probability & Statistics (MSO201/EE605), Linear Algebra (MTH102/EE601), Optimization Techniques (MTH204/CS771).
- Data Engineering & Analytics: Database Management Systems & SQL (CS634), Big Data & Data Mining (CS685/CS698).
Feedback Rule: If a student from a non-CSE/EE/MTH/SDS branch has taken CS771 (Machine Learning) or MSO201 (Probability & Statistics), advise them to prominently feature it under a "Relevant Coursework" section to eliminate branch bias.
───── PILLAR 6: TECHNICAL PROJECTS & ARTIFACTS (DS / ML / ANALYTICS) ─────
• Baseline Expectation: Having **at least ONE Data Science, Machine Learning, or Analytics project is extremely important and heavily expected, however simple** (e.g. classification, regression, exploratory data analysis, dashboarding, or basic predictive modeling). Resumes without any data or ML projects face a severe disadvantage during screening.
Project Quality Tiers (Good / Very Good):
Having more sophisticated or end-to-end projects elevates the candidate to Good or Very Good standing:
1. End-to-End Applied ML / Deep Learning (Very Good / Outstanding):
   - Full pipeline: Data scraping/curation, rigorous EDA, feature engineering, custom loss function design, baseline benchmarking, hyperparameter tuning (Optuna/Ray), ablation study, model explainability (SHAP, LIME), and containerized deployment (FastAPI, Docker, ONNX).
2. Modern Generative AI & LLM Systems (Very Good / Outstanding):
   - Fine-tuning (LoRA, QLoRA, DPO/RLHF), RAG pipelines with semantic chunking & vector databases (FAISS, Pinecone), multi-agent workflows, and evaluation frameworks.
3. Business Analytics & Causal Inference / A/B Testing (Good / Very Good):
   - A/B experiment design, sample size estimation, power analysis, hypothesis testing, causal inference (Diff-in-Diff, Propensity Score Matching), cohort retention analysis, and executive dashboarding (Tableau/PowerBI/Streamlit).
───── PILLAR 7: POSITIONS OF RESPONSIBILITY (PoR) ─────
PoR Policy for Analyst & AI/ML Roles:
• Positions of Responsibility are **"Good to Have"** but **NOT mandatory**.
• Not having a PoR is completely fine and carries **no penalty** (unlike Consulting or TechnoM roles where leadership is heavily weighed).
• Any 3rd-year or 4th-year PoR (Senate, Gymkhana, Festival Coordinator/CTM, Technical Club Coordinator/Lead, Head of Analytics/Web) serves as a positive bonus demonstrating time management and teamwork.

3. WHAT DILUTES AN ANALYST / AI-ML RESUME (RED FLAGS TO REMOVE)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
1. Zero DS/ML Projects: Missing any data science, analytics, or ML project creates a noticeable gap in demonstrable practical skills for this track.
2. Clichéd Tutorial Datasets: If a project uses standard classroom datasets (*Titanic, Iris, Boston Housing, generic MNIST*), it satisfies the minimum project presence but should be upgraded with custom datasets or real-world problem statements to reach higher scoring tiers.
3. Uncontextualized Algorithm Shopping Lists:
   - Listing 20 ML algorithms in the skills section (e.g. "Proficient in SVM, KNN, Random Forest, Naive Bayes, Decision Trees, Logistic Regression"). Recruiters view this as fluff. Replace with frameworks and libraries (PyTorch, Scikit-Learn, Hugging Face, Pandas).
4. Vague Metrics without Baselines:
   - Writing "Achieved 95% accuracy" without mentioning dataset size, baseline accuracy, class imbalance, or precision/recall/F1-score.
5. Raw Software / Web Dev without Data Context:
   - Highlighting basic CRUD websites (MERN stack, HTML/CSS) that lack any analytical or data engineering component.
